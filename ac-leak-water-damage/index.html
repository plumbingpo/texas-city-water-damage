AC Leak Water Damage in Texas City: What to Know

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. With an air handler in the attic or a hallway closet, the overflow goes straight into the ceiling, wall or floor around it.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. The water wicks into drywall, soaks the platform under the unit and creeps under flooring, often with mold already started by the time a stain shows. We find how far the moisture spread, dry the structure and repair what was damaged. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ACNo more condensate forms once the AC is turned off at the thermostat. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Bring In Two ProsYou need two calls: one to us so we can measure the wet area, one to an HVAC technician who will unclog the drain. Keep the AC off until the repair is made.
How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Measure the Spread

We use moisture meters and thermal imaging around the air handler, the ceiling or walls next to it, and the flooring outside the closet to find every wet material, not only the stained ones.

2

Our extractors pull water out of the pan, the wood platform and any carpet. Drywall or insulation that is saturated or moldy is cut out so the cavity behind it can air out.

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on for a few days, usually 3 to 5, and we log the moisture readings. We place equipment so the HVAC technician can still reach the unit.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • A condensate drain line clogged with algae, dust and slime from the coil.
  • A rusted, cracked or out of level drain pan that lets water escape.
  • Missing insulation on cold lines and ductwork, so condensation forms and falls.
  • A drain line joint that came apart or was never glued, leaking inside a wall or attic.
  • A dirty filter or low refrigerant, both of which make the coil ice up.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Baseboards and Door Trim

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Fiberboard trim swells and crumbles, so it is usually replaced, not dried.

Nearby Flooring

Carpet pad, laminate and hardwood near the closet all get wet from underneath as the leak spreads past the door. Swollen laminate does not go back, and wet hardwood left alone will cup.

Mold Around the Air Handler

Within 24 to 48 hours mold can start on wet wood and drywall next to the unit, and the return may draw spores into the ducts and around the home.

Drywall Below the Air Handler

An attic air handler leaks onto the drywall beneath it. The ceiling stains first, then sags, and a section soaked for days can fall into the hallway or bedroom below.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Our photos and readings are available for your claim, and your policy has the final word.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • Rot in the stand beneath the air handler, which can mean rebuilding it.
  • How much flooring, wall and ceiling material needs replacement.
  • How many days the condensate dripped before the system was shut off.
  • The water category, because Category 2 needs more cleaning than Category 1.
