Wet Carpet Drying: What Texas City Homeowners Should Know

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. The face fibers can feel only damp while the pad underneath is soaked like a sponge and the subfloor below it is wet too. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. In Texas City, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. Sewage or outside flood water means carpet and pad both come out.

What It Looks Like

  • As the carpet dries, yellowish or brown stains wick to the top of the pile
  • Carpet that still feels cold and clammy to bare feet days after the spill
  • Soft or squeaky subfloor beneath the area of carpet that was soaked
  • Wet tack strip showing through as dark, rusty streaks next to the baseboard
  • Musty, sour odor that builds whenever the door and windows stay shut
  • Carpet that has come off the tack strip, leaving loose areas or ripples
What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Map the Wet Area

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Pull Out the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry All the Layers

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reset

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Subfloor Damage

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. Weeks later, with the spill long forgotten, you get raised seams, squeaks and soft spots.

Soaked Pad Underneath

Pad can hold many times its weight in water. It keeps feeding moisture into the carpet above and the floor below long after the surface feels dry.

Mold on the Backing

Growth usually starts on the underside of the carpet, where it is dark and damp. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

Water Quality Drops

A clean water spill does not remain clean for long. It collects bacteria and dirt from the pad and fibers, which can turn a simple drying job into a removal.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

Hours 24 to 48

Odor and Mold Begin

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

Past one week

Past Saving

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.
