How Long Drying Takes in a Dallas Home

A surface that feels dry is not proof that a room is dry. The face of drywall often seems normal when the cavity side is damp, and carpet fibers dry long before the padding underneath. For that reason we rely on meters, not on feel. In Dallas we use pin meters that measure moisture inside wood and drywall, non-invasive meters that scan behind surfaces, and thermal cameras that show cool, damp patches.

Different materials let go of water at different speeds. Drywall and carpet give up moisture fast, but studs, subflooring, hardwood and concrete are slow. The layout of dehumidifiers and air movers follows those rates and is changed as each day's numbers come in. Most jobs finish in 3 to 5 days, and if yours will take longer you will hear it from us at the start.

Water Damage We See Most in Dallas

Every house has a few weak points where water tends to get loose. These are the ones Dallas property owners report to us again and again.

Water heaters: Sediment collects in the bottom of a tank and slowly eats through the steel. Look for moisture at the base, rust streaks on the jacket or a small pool that keeps coming back. If the tank ruptures, it keeps refilling and draining onto the floor until someone closes the cold supply valve. Heaters in a hallway closet or attic can ruin flooring and ceilings well beyond the utility area.

Roofs: Wind can lift shingles and drive rain underneath them in a single storm. Leaks also start around chimneys, vent pipes and skylights where the sealant has dried out. Water travels along rafters before it drips, so the wet spot inside may be far from the hole outside. For roof leaks in Dallas, we tarp where needed, pull out soaked insulation and dry the structure so the ceiling can be repaired.

Freezing: Depending on the season, a hard overnight freeze can burst lines that were fine the day before. Outdoor spigots, sprinkler feeds and pipes in crawl spaces or along outside walls are hit first. Vacant homes and rooms with the heat turned down are especially exposed. No flow or a bare trickle at the tap on a frigid day means a frozen line, so close the main and get help.

Backups: A drain that gurgles or several fixtures that slow down together point to a blockage in the main line. When the clog becomes complete, every flush and every load of laundry comes back out downstairs. The water is unsanitary, and hard surfaces need disinfecting after the solids and liquid are removed. Until a plumber opens the line, do not use sinks or toilets and keep everyone clear of the mess.

Seepage: Dampness at the base of a basement or foundation wall often means water is collecting outside. The usual reasons are grading that tilts inward, clogged perimeter drains and downspouts that empty too close. Early clues include powdery white residue on the concrete, flaking wall paint and air that always smells musty. We remove any standing water in Dallas homes, dry the walls and slab, and explain what likely caused it.
