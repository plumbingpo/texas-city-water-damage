Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What Texas City Homeowners Should Know

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

Signs to Look For

  • Wall coverings lifting, blistering or creasing close to floor level
  • New gaps along casings and baseboard tops as swollen material shifts
  • Nail or screw heads popping out and seams showing through the paint
  • A wall that feels cool to the touch compared with others
  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
  • A musty smell that is stronger near one wall or outlet
If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Wicking Begins

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

Hours 24 to 48

Swelling and Blisters

The wet line rises higher, baseboards swell and paint begins to blister. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Day 3 to 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

After a week

Damage Reaches the Studs

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Wet Sheathing Outside

On an exterior wall, water can soak the sheathing behind the insulation. That layer dries slowly and is checked before the wall is closed.

Insulation Holding Water

Once wet, fiberglass insulation stays wet and keeps the studs damp. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

Hidden Behind Cabinets

Sections of wall covered by cabinets, a vanity or shelving stay damp because air never reaches them. We take readings through toe kicks and from the opposite side to find it.

Growth Behind the Paint

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. That side may be coated in growth even as the room side appears spotless.

How We Fix It

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Map the Moisture

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Cut or Ventilate

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs and remaining drywall read dry, typically 3 to 5 days. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.
