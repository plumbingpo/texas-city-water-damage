Kitchen Water Damage in Texas City

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. Our Texas City crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

What It Looks Like

  • An unexplained jump in water use, or dripping you can hear when nothing is running
  • Cupped or raised floorboards, wood or laminate, near the dishwasher door
  • Sticking cabinet doors, or doors whose edges no longer match up
  • The bottom edges of cabinet side panels swelling or flaking apart
  • Along the cabinet line or beside the fridge, vinyl that feels soft or looks stained
  • Water stains or bubbled paint on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher
What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Stop the Source

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. It is common for water beneath a kitchen floor to spread further than you would guess.

3

Dry Without Demolition

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Remove Only Damage

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first hour

Water Goes Underneath

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Day 3 to 7

Floors Begin Moving

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Refrigerator Water Line

A few drops per minute can escape from the narrow tube that supplies the ice maker. Behind a fridge that never moves, that is enough to rot flooring and the wall behind.

Flooring Over Flooring

It is common for a kitchen floor to be laid right over the previous one. Between those layers water is trapped, unable to evaporate and invisible from above.

Drips at the Sink Drain

A loose drain fitting or disposal seal leaks only while the sink is draining, so it is easy to miss. A damp sink cabinet smells and grows mold quickly because that water has food waste in it.

Behind the Dishwasher

Slowly dripping supply or drain fittings soak the cabinet sides beside the unit and the subfloor below. None of it is visible behind the appliance until the flooring starts to change.
